He developed the psychobiological model of exercise.
This model suggests that your muscles do not actually stop you when you are tired.
Instead, your brain acts as a central governor.
It constantly monitors your effort and creates the feeling of fatigue to protect your body from damage.
If you have a powerful reason to keep going, your brain recalculates the cost of energy.
It releases its grip on your safety reserves.
Did the fear of a World Cup exit unlock a chemical override in the Argentine players?

We must look to the research of Doctor Geir Jordet.
He spent years studying elite soccer players under pressure.
Jordet discovered that under extreme stress, most players experience cognitive tunnel vision.
Their eyes freeze.

But elite performers do the opposite.
They scan the field more frequently in the final seconds than at any other time.
How does the brain maintain this intense visual control when every instinct is screaming to panic?
This brings us to the research of Doctor Christian Swann.
He studied the difference between flow states and clutch states.
A flow state is effortless and automatic.
You feel it when everything is going perfectly.
But a clutch state is deliberate and intense.
It occurs when you realize that failure is imminent and you must act now.
Your brain actively pushes through the physical fatigue.
It focuses entirely on the immediate goal.
The Argentine team did not win by accident.
They entered a collective clutch state where the pressure became fuel.
Why do some teams fold under this pressure while others thrive?
