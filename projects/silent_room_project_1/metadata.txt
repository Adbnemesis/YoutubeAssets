## Video Description
Have you ever tried sitting in a perfectly quiet room with absolutely no distractions? It sounds peaceful, but for most of us, it quickly becomes terrifying. 

In this video, we explore the fascinating psychology and neuroscience behind why we fear silence. From the famous University of Virginia experiment—where 67% of men chose to give themselves painful electric shocks rather than sit alone with their thoughts—to the discovery of the Default Mode Network (the brain's "dark energy"), we break down why our minds are so desperately afraid of quiet. 

We'll also look at how our modern addiction to digital stimulation has ruined our ability to tolerate boredom, how our prehistoric evolutionary instincts keep us on high alert in the quiet, and how letting your mind wander is actually the secret soil where original thought and creativity grow.

The next time you reach for your phone to kill a quiet second... stop. You might just be running away from the most complex machine in the known universe because you fear the dark.
