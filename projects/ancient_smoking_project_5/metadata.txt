## Video Description
Tonight, you sit by a crackling campfire and watch the smoke rise. You instinctively turn away from the sting, but thousands of years ago, your ancestors did the exact opposite. They leaned in and inhaled the toxic fumes directly into their lungs—and the reason why is stranger than you think.

In this video, we explore the bizarre history and science of ancient smoking. From Shannon Tushingham’s discovery of sumac residues on ancient North American pipes to Meng Ren’s excavation of 2,500-year-old cannabis burners in the Pamir Mountains, you will discover that inhaling smoke was rarely about recreation. Anthropologist Ed Hagen suggests that your ancestors smoked to survive, using plant toxins like nicotine and THC to fumigate parasites eating them from the inside. Discover how this sacred, survival tool evolved into a modern addiction.
