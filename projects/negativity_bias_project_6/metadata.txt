## Video Description
Tonight, when you lie in bed, your mind will start to play a dangerous game. The room is perfectly safe. Yet, your mind is racing with every awkward moment from your day, replaying a slight frown from a coworker or worrying about a bill that is not even due. Why does your brain do this? Why is it so easy to spiral into worry, but so hard to hold onto a compliment?

In this video, we explore the evolutionary science and psychology behind the "Negativity Bias"—an ancient survival mechanism that programs us to expect the worst. Discover how our ancestors' threat-detection systems hijack our modern happiness, featuring the ground-breaking research of Dr. Paul Rozin and the spider-cake experiments, Dr. John Cacioppo's brain wave tracking, Dr. Rick Hanson's Velcro vs. Teflon metaphor, and Dr. Roy Baumeister's discovery that bad is simply stronger than good. Learn how you can balance the scales, build your cognitive superpower, and reprogram your neural networks.
