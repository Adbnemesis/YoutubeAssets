Sample video title - 
Do Wild Animals Know When a Human Is Trying to Help Them?

Sample video description - 
To a wild animal, a human isn't a neighbor. It isn't neutral.

It's a predator. The most dangerous one that has ever existed.

So what happens when that predator shows up to help?

This video gets into the science behind one of the strangest things researchers have documented in the field — the moment a trapped animal stops fighting, goes completely still, and lets a human work around its body. What's happening inside its nervous system during that moment, why it happens in some animals and not others, and what some of them do in the seconds after they're freed that nobody has a clean explanation for.

The humpback whale near the Farallon Islands that held still for over an hour while divers cut ropes from its body — and what it did the moment the last rope was cut. The wolf that stopped lunging the moment a researcher held eye contact, then stood and looked back before disappearing into the tree line. And the neurological discovery that made scientists stop dismissing these encounters entirely.

The science has a name for what happens when a threat classification system runs out of framework. What it can't answer is what the animal is actually experiencing when it realizes it's free.
