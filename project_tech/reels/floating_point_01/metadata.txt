Why does a billion-dollar supercomputer fail grade-school math? 🤯

Open your browser console and type:
0.1 + 0.2
And you'll get: 0.30000000000000004

Here’s why it actually happens:

1️⃣ Binary Base-2 Representation
Computers don't think in decimal (base-10). In binary (base-2), the number 0.1 is an infinite repeating fraction (0.0001100110011...).

2️⃣ IEEE-754 53-Bit Truncation
Computer memory isn't infinite. The CPU cuts off and rounds the fraction at 53 bits.

3️⃣ Compounding Approximations
When you add two rounded binary approximations together, the tiny truncation errors compound—leaving that extra 4 at the 17th decimal place!

Your computer didn't get math wrong. It just ran out of bits! ⚡
